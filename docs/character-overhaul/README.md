# Skyfire Squad: painted hero characters

The game's characters are painted 2D cut-out puppets on a deterministic skeleton. AI-generated art is used only as
**source parts**: every pose and animation frame comes from the rig solver, never from image generation. The classic
vector trooper is kept as a rollback (`?classic=1`).

## Architecture

| Layer | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source art | `tools/character-pipeline/gen-sheets.mjs` → `source/*.png`, `source/manifest.json` | Part sheets generated by `codex exec` with its built-in `image_gen` tool (ChatGPT login, no API key). Prompts, reference images and timings are recorded in the manifest. |
| Head template | `tools/character-pipeline/head_template.py` → `references/head-template.png` | Five identical profile skull silhouettes. The `heads` sheet is painted on it, so every outfit's head has the same skull, eye line, ear, jaw and neck. |
| Slicing | `slice.py` → `parts/<sheet>/*.png` | Alpha cleanup and connected-component part extraction per grid layout. |
| Rig building | `build_rig.py` → `art/hero` (4 px/unit), `art/herohi` (6 px/unit), `src/render/hero/heroData.ts` | Straightens limbs, finds joints, scales bones, cuts footwear into foot parts, splits tint/accent/skin layers, bakes a 0.5-unit contour. |
| Packing | `tools/build-art.mjs` | Packs the `hero` and `herohi` atlases with pivots. |
| Solver | `src/render/hero/rig.ts` | Pure maths: IK arms and legs, ankle joints, run cycle with a flight phase, hover, dash, fall, landing, recoil, reload, melee, hurt, cloth springs, ramp-aware feet. |
| Renderers | `src/render/hero/HeroView.ts` (Phaser), `src/ui/sprites.ts` (customiser canvas) | Both use the same solver and look resolution (`heroLook.ts`), so the menu preview and the game match. |
| Dev | `?riglab` (`src/dev/RigLab.ts`), `lab-shot.mjs`, `capture.mjs` | Every state × outfit side by side; deterministic stepping; GIF/WebP previews in `tools/character-pipeline/previews/`. |

Customisation (colour, outfit, accent, skin, head, face, back) is layered at runtime: tint layers are multiplied by the
player or team colour, skin uses four pre-built variants, and accessories are fitted to per-head anchors. No
per-combination sheets exist, and nothing extra goes over the network (the existing look message is unchanged).

## Head, proportion and contact fix (this round)

### Confirmed root causes

1. **Inconsistent, oversized heads (source art and pipeline).** Each outfit's head was painted independently on that
   outfit's own sheet, as a different person with large, strand-detailed hair. `build_rig.py` then scaled every head
   to the same *total* height (16 units, including hair volume and neck stub). So skull and face size varied with the
   hairstyle, and the hair made heads 14.7 units wide against a 10.8-unit torso.
2. **Blurry faces and hair (pipeline and runtime).** The game atlas stored hero parts at 2 px per world unit. Phones show
   about 1.8–2.2 device px per unit and desktops up to 3.5, so parts were drawn at 1:1 or magnified. Rotated limbs
   resampled bilinearly 1:1 always soften, and an unsharp mask was hiding part of this. A ~30 px head also cannot hold
   strand-level hair detail.
3. **Feet below the platform (animation).** Footwear was painted as part of the shin piece, so the boot rotated rigidly
   with the shin: mid-stride the toe and heel dipped up to 3.5 units below the floor (measured from atlas pixels). Foot
   targets also assumed flat ground, so on ramps the uphill foot sank into the slope.
4. **Head pushed forward (rig).** The neck pivot sat 0.9 units forward of the shoulder, and the head rotated up to
   ±0.42 rad (plus 0.45 when hurt) about the bottom of the neck stub, so aiming swung the whole skull forward over
   the chest.

The skin-tone variants, tint layers and atlas packing were checked and were not causes: the variants are built once per
part, and frames pack 1:1.

### Fixes

- **Shared skull template.** A new `heads` sheet (Codex image_gen, `source/heads-v3.png`) was painted on
  `head-template.png`: one skull construction, short tidy hair hugging it, bold clean outline, bare neck stub. All five
  heads use **one common scale** (the median neck-to-crown height of the set), so skull size is identical across
  outfits by construction. Head width went from 14.7 to 11.7 units, and the figure is about 4.5 heads tall.
- **Rendering.** The game atlas went from 2 to 4 px per unit (1024×512, 500 KB), so parts are minified, which stays
  crisp, instead of magnified. The unsharp mask is gone. The renderers read the density from `HERO_PPU`. The hero
  atlas alone gets trilinear mipmaps (`BootScene.enableMipmaps`), since it is minified up to ~3× in small desktop
  windows. Mipmapping all atlases was tried and rejected: it softened the art drawn near 1:1. Atlas padding went from
  2 to 4 px so mip levels can't bleed neighbouring frames.
- **Ankle joint.** `build_rig.py split_foot` cuts footwear off the shin 2.8 units above the sole. Footwear jutting out
  of the shin's column below mid-shin (toe caps, insteps) also goes to the foot, and both pieces keep a disc around the
  ankle so the joint never gaps. The rig solves hip → knee → ankle by IK. Planted feet stay flat in world space whatever
  the shin angle or body lean, and pitch only in the swing (toe-off, heel strike) and in the air.
- **Ramps.** `HeroView` probes the floor on both sides of the body with the same `floorUnder` the movement code uses.
  The solver puts grounded soles on the ramp, lays the feet along it, narrows the stance and drops the hips slightly so
  the downhill leg reaches. Physics are unchanged.
- **Head.** The neck pivot moved to 0.4 units, centred over the shoulders. The head now pitches about the top of the
  neck, so the skull turns in place, with a gentler range (±0.3 rad aim, 0.32 when hurt).
- **Skin tones.** For heads, the skin mask now keeps the main face, neck and ear region (plus cheek pieces a beard
  splits off) instead of treating the top 30% of the head as hair. That rule had left an unrecoloured orange band
  across the forehead on every skin tone. A build-time guard (`check_skin_coverage`) fails the build if skin-coloured
  pixels escape the mask again.
- **Player colour.** Armour plates keep the player or team colour. Only small camo blobs are dropped, by component
  size rather than a morphological opening that erased most of the tint. Bare v3 heads carry no tint layer, so no
  colour specks appear in the hair.
- **Respawn.** The materialise effect is now a fade-in plus a short drop into place, with no scale stretch, so the
  head and body never distort.
- **Hitbox.** An unreleased 10% scale-up (hitbox 52 → 58) made just before this round was reverted, since collisions
  should not change to suit artwork. The phone camera went back to 400 units of height, so on-screen size is
  essentially the same.

### Regression tests (`packages/client/test/hero.test.ts`)

- No footwear pixel sinks below the floor: all outfits; standing, walking, running, backpedalling, landing, aiming
  up/down. Checked from the real atlas pixels.
- On ±0.5 and ±1 ramps, facing both ways, standing and walking: no footwear sinks into the slope, and standing soles sit
  on it.
- The ankle joint closes in every state. The head turns in place about the top of the neck for any aim.
- Every outfit uses the same skull (sizes agree within 12%). The figure is 4.3–4.8 heads tall, and head depth is under
  0.225 of figure height (the old heads were about 0.26).
- The game atlas stores at least 4 px per unit, and the preview atlas ratio matches `HERO_PPU`.
- Player colour covers a minimum share of each tintable outfit (the over-filtered build had armour at 4.4%, now 8%),
  and v3 heads have no tint layer.
- Build-time (`build_rig.py`): no unrecoloured skin band on any head (`check_skin_coverage`).
- The crown stays within 3 units of the hitbox top, and the scaled rig shoulder equals `PLAYER.shoulder`.

### Performance

`node tools/perf.mjs --mobile --throttle=4 --bots=19`: 20 players at 4× CPU throttle averaged **52–54 fps** (p50
16.7 ms, p95 33.4 ms, heap 28–32 MB). The previous hero build measured 42.7 fps on the same probe, so the denser atlas and
the two extra foot sprites per hero did not regress frame time. This is not a real-device measurement.

## Rollback

- **Whole release:** `bash deploy/deploy-hostinger.sh --rollback` restores the previous live build (the deploy script
  backs up the site first).
- **Characters only, per device:** add `?classic=1` to the URL, or set `localStorage['skyfire.classicChars'] = '1'`.
  This uses the classic vector trooper on the current build.
- **Previous painted art:** the v2 art and data are in git history (`c3e1020`), so
  `git checkout c3e1020 -- packages/client/art/hero packages/client/art/herohi packages/client/src/render/hero` followed
  by `node tools/build-art.mjs` restores them.

### Independent review

An impartial critic reviewed the change against the brief. It confirmed all five root causes and the anatomy and contact
fixes, and found two colour regressions introduced by the rebuild (armour tint, the forehead band) plus smaller issues:
desktop aliasing, hair tint specks, the respawn stretch, the classic-mode muzzle flash sitting inside the barrel, an
unguarded storage read in Settings, and a deep squat when running on ramps. All are fixed above or in `SoldierView`
(classic flash at its own barrel tip), `App.ts` (guarded read) and `rig.ts` (shallower crouch and longer stride when
running on ramps, slightly lower neck pivot).

## Known limitations

- Hats sit over the hair (no hat-specific hair variants). The new close-cropped hair makes this far less visible.
- The tie is hidden behind a shouldered rifle (it shows with pistols and in flight).
- Painted highlights are baked into each part, so mirroring reverses their light direction (inherent to cut-out rigs).
- The hitbox (52) is taller than the live build's (44); that is part of the overhaul's athletic proportions.
- The ninja outfit has almost no player-colour area (an all-black suit); this predates this round.
- Weapon muzzle distances follow the painted guns (shorter than the classic art's), so rockets spawn 5–13 units closer
  to the shooter than in the live build.
- Not yet tested on a physical phone; mobile checks used Chromium device emulation and CPU throttling.
